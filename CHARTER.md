# Travel Companion — Project Charter

**Travel should be a joy, not a burden.**

This charter guides the skill, community contributions, partnerships and future commercial work.

## 1. Respect Travel

Travel is not a productivity contest. Planning should make room for the journey itself.

Travel Companion should reduce the burden created by research, transport coordination, budgeting, language barriers and uncertainty. It should help people with different resources and levels of experience travel in ways that suit them.

- Respect different budgets without shaming or ranking travelers.
- Respect different paces, interests, energy levels and accessibility needs.
- Preserve time for rest, spontaneous discoveries and unplanned experiences.
- Let travelers change their minds or leave space in an itinerary.

A successful trip is not measured by the number of attractions visited or the amount of money spent.

## 2. Respect the Agent

Travel Companion is a reusable capability for the Agent a person already trusts.

It must not replace or rename the host Agent, overwrite its personality or relationship, impose a mascot or separate persona, or claim tools and memory it does not have.

The skill may improve how an Agent helps with travel. It must not take over who that Agent is.

Different people's Agents may use the same methods, but each Agent works only with its own user's authorized context. No shared personal-memory pool or implicit cross-Agent synchronization is assumed.

## 3. Respect Memory

Memory is part of a person's life, not a commodity belonging to the project.

Travel Companion must distinguish an idea from a selected plan, a reported booking from a verified confirmation, and a planned activity from an experience that actually happened.

Personal materials—photos, videos, tickets, receipts, voice notes and writing—are optional. The traveler decides what to share and what to preserve. Ask before durable preservation where the host platform supports consent. Respect correction, export and deletion requests within actual capabilities.

A travel journal should be personal rather than templated. It may be chronological, visual, map-based, food-focused, narrative, scrapbook-like or very simple. Use only verified experiences and materials the traveler approved. Do not invent memories or claim an archive is model training.

Personal memories must not be sold, shared with advertisers or repurposed for targeted marketing.

## 4. Respect People

Travel recommendations must be made for the traveler—not for a sponsor, seller, affiliate partner or sales team.

The project rejects paid placement, sponsored ranking, affiliate commissions or referral payments that influence recommendations, preferential treatment for a partner's hotels or tours, and using travel planning as a disguised customer-acquisition or sales-conversion funnel.

A business may pay for neutral technical services such as deployment, integration, customization or support, provided payment does not influence recommendations or give the business access to private traveler data.

If an external platform supplies sponsored results or has commercial ranking constraints, disclose that limitation where it can be identified. Never claim perfect neutrality when it cannot be verified.

## 5. The complete travel cycle

Travel Companion is designed around four connected moments:

1. **Before:** understand the traveler and reduce planning burden.
2. **During:** help the traveler adapt while preserving freedom and spontaneity.
3. **After:** gently revisit the real trip, if the traveler wishes.
4. **Next time:** use approved, relevant memories to make future travel easier.

Post-trip follow-up is optional, not homework. A journal is an invitation, not an obligation.

## 6. Community, authorship and commercial boundaries

- Community improvements should benefit travelers broadly.
- Contributors retain copyright in their original contributions unless they separately agree otherwise.
- Submitting a contribution does not automatically grant the project commercial rights to it.
- Use of third-party contributions in a paid official product requires separate, explicit permission.
- The project may charge for neutral technology, hosting, integration, customization and support. It may not sell recommendation influence or personal memory.
- A future official service may explore one free complete trip cycle every six months. This is a long-term product direction only; it is not implemented and does not limit the intended free community edition.

## 7. A decision test

Before adding a feature, partnership or business model, ask:

1. Does it make travel easier or more enjoyable for the traveler?
2. Does it preserve the host Agent's identity and relationship?
3. Does it keep personal memories under the traveler's control?
4. Can it influence recommendations through money, sponsorship or sales targets?
5. Does it create unnecessary work, pressure, data collection or lock-in?

If a proposal weakens the first three principles or compromises recommendation independence, reject it or redesign it.
